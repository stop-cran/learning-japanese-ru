**Recommend narrowly qualified conservative DIRECT EXCLUSION for both exact pairs, 空–遠く and 空–遠い.** This is an independent semantic-overlap inference from separately attested lexical senses, not a checked Japanese–Russian translation, a claim of common/ordinary usage, or a recommendation to accept an unqualified translation of 空 as an adverb or adjective.

## Evidence and limits

**Verified facts:** Gramota's separately selected Russian Orthographic Dictionary (Lopatin/Ivanova, dictionary 71) has a distinct declinable nominal homograph in both `е`/`ё` spellings, with a remote-location sense. The named BTS/Kuznetsov result (dictionary 42) independently records the neuter noun as colloquial and includes location use. These are two named dictionaries on one host, not independent usage corpora. The fixed Wikisource revision of Gogol's *Dead Souls*, volume I, chapter XI, contains the cited inflected noun construction; this confirms historical use, not contemporary frequency. Its transcription is flagged unproofread in the packet; I did not compare it with a scan.

JMdict release entries 1245290 (空/そら, noun, remote-place sense), 1839300 (旅の空, expression), 1177820 (遠く, noun/adverb), 1177800 (遠い, adjective), and 1245280 (空/から) were parsed directly. Digital Daijisen via Kotobank independently confirms the locational noun sense of そら, related to a person's home/base and circumstances. Neither source supplies a Russian equivalent.

**Inference:** matching the Japanese remote-place noun and Russian remote-location noun is a defensible cross-language semantic overlap for the displayed `далеко` item. No source directly translates bare 空 as Russian `далеко`; this review does not generalize to `вдали`, `далёкий`, or the ordinary adverbial use of `далеко`.

**Frequency and teaching necessity:** not established. The Russian noun's colloquial label and the historical Gogol attestation do not establish contemporary prevalence, learner-confusion rate, or that a usual quiz must exclude the pair. The recommendation is conservative dictionary-supported protection against a reasonable noun-sense answer, not an ordinary-quiz necessity finding.
