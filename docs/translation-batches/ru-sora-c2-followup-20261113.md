## Material new evidence

The exact Russian caption component **далеко** is not only an adverb/predicate.
Two named dictionaries explicitly admit **далеко́** as a declinable noun alongside
**далёко**, with a remote-location meaning. This is positive evidence absent from
review 004, not another argument from dictionary omission.

1. **R1 — Russian Orthographic Dictionary**, edited by V. V. Lopatin and
   O. E. Ivanova, [Gramota, exact lookup, dictionary 71](https://gramota.ru/poisk?query=%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D0%BE&mode=slovari&dicts%5B%5D=71).
   Inspect nominal headword **далеко́2 / далёко2**, separately from adverb 1.
   The entry supplies both paradigms and identifies a spatial nominal sense.
   The generic Metadictionary summary below is not the full dictionary result.
2. **R2 — BTS/Kuznetsov**, [Gramota, exact lookup, dictionary 42](https://gramota.ru/poisk?query=%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D0%BE&mode=slovari&dicts%5B%5D=42).
   A neuter nominal subentry follows sections I-II and precedes the entry with
   *за*. It is marked colloquial and covers a remote location as well as future
   reference. It supplies usage examples, including an attributed Gogol citation.
   R1 and R2 are distinct dictionaries on one host, not two independent corpora.
3. **R3 — actual attributed usage:** Gogol, *Dead Souls*, volume I, chapter XI
   (1842), [Wikisource revision 5640336](https://ru.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=5640336):
   > Русь! Русь! вижу тебя, из моего чудного, прекрасного далека тебя вижу

   This is an inflected noun with modifiers, not an invented example. It supports
   historical location usage; R1/R2 establish the nominative paradigm. The
   transcription is marked unproofread; no scan or modern frequency was checked.
   This is the original public-domain Russian text, not a translation of Japanese.

**J1/J2 — Japanese side:** the directly parsed JMdict archive again confirms
1245290, 空/そら, sense 3, noun: “far-off place; distant place.”
[Digital Daijisen's そら noun sense 3](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%A9%BA-54809)
independently describes a place remote from a person's home/base, also circumstance.
Its examples do not prove an exhaustive construction restriction.

**Author inference, not a quoted bilingual translation:** the attested Russian
remote-location noun is a plausible semantic match for this Japanese noun sense.
It is the **same literal далеко component in each actual caption**:

| Word | Actual title |
|---|---|
| 空 | небо |
| 遠く | далеко; вдали |
| 遠い | далёкий; далеко |

This offers a concrete basis for **considering conservative direct exclusions**,
separately for the two pairs. It does **not** establish bare 空 as the ordinary
adverb *далеко*, adverb *вдали* or adjective *далёкий*. It does not claim that 空
and 遠い are general synonyms, or use the existing 遠く–遠い guard transitively.

**Confidence:** high in the Russian nominal attestation and Japanese lexical sense;
medium in the proposed conservative caption-overlap inference. No new checked
bilingual source directly translates bare 空 as the Russian noun. No contemporary
frequency, learner-confusion rate or ordinary-quiz necessity is established.
The review must decide whether these facts suffice for a narrowly qualified
conservative exclusion or remain a promising but insufficient lead.

The full captions matter: *вдали* and *далёкий* favour ordinary distance readings.
A reviewer must not silently replace the actual titles with noun-only labels.
Nor should dictionary omission, grammar alone or unverified frequency settle C2.
This follows the current RU instruction distinguishing conservative
dictionary-supported protection from ordinary-quiz necessity.
